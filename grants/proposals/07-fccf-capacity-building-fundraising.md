### Description of the capacity building request

The Bridge Foundation for Student Athletes is a Stamford based 501(c)(3) founded in 2023. Bridge expands college access for underserved student athletes through hands on recruiting guidance, college lists, coach outreach, player profiles, timeline planning, and academic preparation, an access fund that covers the showcase fees, camp fees, and equipment costs that end recruiting journeys before they start, and Pathways Finance, an eight week financial literacy program for teens. This year Bridge serves 35 athletes. Since 2019, the founder's work has guided more than 30 student athletes to college, and five committed in September 2026 alone.

Our programs are growing faster than our fundraising. Bridge's 2026 operating budget is $32,750, with about three months of cash on hand, and nearly all revenue comes from board giving and individual donors raised through the founder's personal network. Board members commit $10,000 each per year, $40,000 a year across the board's four giving members, and have paid $32,666.77 to date. Bridge has no development staff, no donor management system in use today, and until now no grants program. In October 2026 Bridge submitted its first foundation request, a letter of inquiry to the New Canaan Community Foundation, and it is building a grants program now. Fundraising that runs on one person's spare hours caps how many athletes Bridge can serve.

This project builds the fundraising operation Bridge needs. Over 12 months starting January 2027 we will: (1) launch a working grants program with prospect research, a submission calendar, and professionally prepared applications; (2) implement HighLevel as our donor management system, so gifts, pledges, and deadlines are tracked in one place and nothing falls through the cracks; and (3) train the board to fundraise with confidence, tied to quarterly check ins on board giving. The goal is plain: diversify revenue beyond one person's network, follow through on board commitments, and win the first institutional grants that make larger ones possible.

### Design elements

Structure and scheduling: A 12 month project, January through December 2027. Months 1 and 2: implement HighLevel for donor tracking and build a prospect list of local and national education and youth sports funders. Months 3 and 4: board fundraising training for the board. Months 5 through 12: rolling grant research, drafting, and submissions, with quarterly board giving check ins against the board's annual commitments. Dave Fisher is the contact for funder calls and reviews every application before it goes out.

Content: Grant prospect research and a 12 month submission calendar. Application drafting with Dave's review of every submission. Donor acknowledgment and reporting templates. A board giving plan tied to the board's annual commitments, with quarterly follow up.

Who participates: Dave Fisher leads the project and works directly with the grant program contractor. The Treasurer maintains Bridge's financial records and will oversee gift and pledge records once the donor system is in place. The full board takes part in the fundraising training and the quarterly giving check ins.

How success will be measured: We will measure applications submitted, grant dollars awarded, board pledge fulfillment, growth in the donor base, and revenue mix, meaning the share of revenue from institutional grants versus individual gifts. Baseline as of November 2026: one institutional application submitted and none yet awarded, and no donor management system in use. (If Alfred's FCCF and MLB YDF requests go in before this is entered, the baseline count changes. TO CONFIRM 8.)

Other capacity needs on the horizon: Evaluation and impact measurement systems that bring athlete outcome tracking into one place, and continued work on financial management tools as the books grow. Bridge already tracks the outcomes funders ask about, including awards, dollars disbursed, showcases and camps attended, college commitments, and scholarship dollars earned.

### Sustainability

This grant pays for one time capacity, not ongoing costs. Once the grants program, the donor system and the board training are in place, they run on staff and board time Bridge already has. The revenue the new program brings in funds whatever tools or support Bridge still needs after the grant period. Bridge has no endowment. Its operating reserve is its bank balance, built from board giving and individual donors.
