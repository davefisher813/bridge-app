## A correction to the earlier draft

Alfred's draft and the Nov 6 sweep describe a "$299 program fee" that Bridge would pay. On Good Sports' own page, $299 is something else: one of the minimum standards for eligibility is that the applicant "charge a participation fee of $299 or below" to the youth it serves. It is a limit on what Bridge charges athletes and families, not a fee Bridge pays. What Bridge pays, per the page, is a shipping and handling fee equal to 5 percent of the value of the equipment donated, some exceptions may apply. This draft is built on the page. Whether Bridge charges any participation fee, and how much, is TO CONFIRM 1 and is an eligibility question.

## 2. The organization

The Bridge Foundation for Student Athletes, based in Stamford, Connecticut, helps underserved student athletes get to college through baseball. Bridge was founded in 2023 by Dave Fisher, who founded the Elite Squad NY travel baseball organization in 2019. Bridge pairs hands on recruiting guidance, college lists, coach outreach, player profiles, timelines and academic preparation with the Access Fund, which pays the showcase, camp and equipment costs that end recruiting journeys before they start. This year Bridge serves 35 athletes. Since 2019 this work has guided more than 30 athletes to college, and five committed in September 2026 alone.

Contact: Dave Fisher, Executive Director, dave@bffsa.org, 203-536-9118. 54 Clifford Ave, Stamford, CT 06905. EIN 99-2660039. 501(c)(3). Website bffsa.org. Founded 2023.

## 3. The need

Equipment is one of the three costs that end Bridge's athletes' recruiting journeys, with showcase fees of $300 to $500 each and camp fees of $200 to $300 each. On Bridge's approved record equipment runs $100 to $200 per athlete, and the Access Fund covers it for families who cannot. Travel baseball costs an average of $3,700 per year nationally and up to $5,000 per year in the Northeast (Aspen Institute Project Play figures, checked October 13; exact citation TO CONFIRM 5). For low income families, the price of taking part alone can decide whether a student is seen by college coaches.

Donated equipment would put gear in the hands of Bridge athletes and free Access Fund dollars for the showcase and camp fees that donated gear cannot cover.

## 4. The request

Equipment donation to Bridge Foundation For Student Athletes Inc. Items, sizes and quantities: TO CONFIRM 6. Bridge's record names equipment only as a cost range per athlete, so no list is invented here.

Cost to Bridge, stated openly: shipping and handling at 5 percent of the value of the equipment donated. There is no cash grant and no project budget in this request. If Good Sports offers a donation, the 5 percent is the only cost. Dave's approval to pay it is TO CONFIRM 7.

## 5. Design elements

Structure and scheduling: Athletes apply for Access Fund support. Dave Fisher and board members review each application, and the board votes. Equipment would reach athletes through the same process. Delivery timing depends on Good Sports and on shipping address (TO CONFIRM 8).

Content: new equipment, apparel or footwear for baseball athletes, as the final list specifies.

Who participates: Bridge athletes, 35 this year. How many would use donated gear in the first season is TO CONFIRM 9.

How success will be measured: Good Sports requires an evaluation after every donation. Bridge already tracks awards, dollars disbursed, showcases and camps attended, college commitments and scholarship dollars earned, and will record which athletes received donated equipment and what it was used for.

## 6. Sustainability

Equipment is not an ongoing cost of this request. Bridge's board commits $10,000 per member per year, $40,000 a year across the four giving members, and Bridge is building a grants program. The Access Fund continues to cover equipment costs for families who cannot. Donated equipment reduces what the Access Fund must spend on gear.
