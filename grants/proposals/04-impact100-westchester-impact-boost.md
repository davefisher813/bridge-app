## What the sweep got wrong, now corrected at the source

The sweep warned that Impact100 has hard gates: 36 months in operation, two years of CPA audited or reviewed financial statements, and revenue between $250,000 and $30,000,000. Those are the Eligibility page's rules for the Transformational Project Grant, the large grant. They do not apply to the Impact Boost Award. The Boost page lists its own, lighter criteria (below). Bridge does not apply for the Transformational Grant, and the Boost rules state an applicant must not have applied for one this cycle.

## 2. The organization

The Bridge Foundation for Student Athletes, based at 54 Clifford Ave, Stamford, Connecticut, helps underserved student athletes get to college. It was founded in 2023 by Dave Fisher, and this year it serves 35 athletes in Stamford, Connecticut, Port Chester, New York, and the Bronx. Bridge runs three programs: hands on recruiting guidance (college lists, coach outreach, player profiles, timelines and academic preparation); the Access Fund, which pays the showcase, camp and equipment costs that end recruiting journeys; and Pathways Finance, an eight week financial literacy program for teens. Since 2019 the founder's work has guided more than 30 athletes to college, and five committed in September 2026 alone.

Contact: Dave Fisher, Executive Director, dave@bffsa.org, 203-536-9118. Website bffsa.org.

## 3. The need

Bridge's own books show what a summer of college recruiting costs: about $3,700 on average and up to $5,000 (Bridge's recorded summer season college recruiting expenses, per Dave, Nov 7; whether that is per athlete or per family, which season, and how many athletes it covers is TO CONFIRM 5). For low income families that price alone decides who is seen by college coaches. Showcases cost $300 to $500 each, camps $200 to $300 each, and equipment $100 to $200. Money is only half the barrier: the recruiting process is complicated and built on relationships, so families without guidance or money are shut out before it starts. Port Chester athletes face the same costs as the rest of Bridge's athletes.

Bridge is a small organization whose fundraising runs on one person's network. A $5,000 award is a large share of a $32,750 annual budget.

## 4. The request

Amount: $5,000, which would fund two Access Fund awards of up to $2,500 each for Port Chester student athletes. Each award covers what the athlete actually needs across showcases, camps and equipment. Athletes apply, Dave Fisher and board members review each application, and the board votes on funding. Every funded athlete also receives Bridge's full recruiting curriculum and college advisory support.

## 6. Sustainability

An award is one time support for two athletes. Bridge has no endowment. Board members commit $10,000 each per year, $40,000 a year across four giving members. Bridge is building a grants program, and the Access Fund continues after any single award.
